it to benchmark large-language-model proposal policies under an identical deterministic validation

I. INTRODUCTION
The observed acceleration of the Universe is one of the
clearest indications that either the cosmic energy budget
or the gravitational sector is not yet fully understood.
The minimalΛCDM model gives a compact effective de-
scription of current background data, but it treats the
late-time vacuum-energy scale as an input and does not
explain whether the acceleration is produced by a rigid
cosmologicalconstant, additionalfields, ormodifiedgrav-
itational dynamics. Scalar-tensor and higher-curvature
theories offer a controlled language for testing this possi-
bility, but they also create a search problem: once curva-
ture invariants, covariant derivatives, and multiple scalar
fields are allowed, the space of covariant Lagrangians is
far too large to inspect manually [1–3].
Most points in this large theory space are not physi-
cally acceptable. Generic higher-derivative theories con-
tain Ostrogradsky modes [4]; many proposed covariant
densities do not admit a regular FLRW initial-value
problem; many numerically regular branches violate the
modified Friedmann constraint during integration; and
most surviving backgrounds fit DES supernova and DESI
BAO distances poorly. A useful autonomous search must
therefore be physics-first: an agent may propose struc-
tures, but deterministic symbolic and numerical code
must decide whether the proposal is legal, stable at the
background-filter level, numerically solvable, and empir-
ically competitive.
The framework used here follows this division of la-
bor. A proposal model chooses a scalar configuration, a
grammar-constrained set of covariant operators, and an
explicit list of parameter samples. The proposal is then
compiled into an isolated Lagrangian and passed through
the same pipeline for every agent: symbolic FLRW vari-
ation, equation-level cancellation of independent third-
and higher-time derivatives, Builder construction of a
dimensionless initial-value problem, Solver integration,
modified Friedmann-constraint monitoring, and compar-
ison with DES-SN and DESI-DR2 BAO data [5, 6]. The
AI system does not estimateχ 2, bypass the grammar,
or override a failed physics stage. This design is close in
spirit to formal-language approaches to autonomous the-
ory discovery, but the grammar and validation stages are
those ofmodified gravity rather thanparticle interactions
[7].
The present paper has two goals. First, it summarizes
the covariant theory space, FLRW derivative-order fil-
ter, constraint logic, dimensionless cosmological system,
and sampling prescription used in the current code base.
These ingredients define what is meant by an accepted
background branch. Second, it reports a matched five-
model benchmark of the proposal layer. We compare
GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen 3.7
Plus, and DeepSeek V4 Pro on the same cold-start task,
with two discovery sectors: an open-search sector that
may include the Einstein–Hilbert anchor, and a non-
EH sector that forbids the bareRterm and searches
for new-physics backgrounds without explicit EH sup-
port. The comparison is organized around global opti-
mum quality, new-physics discovery, speed/efficiency, ro-
bustness/stability, and acceptance rate.
The results should be interpreted with care. The back-
ground data strongly favor EH-anchored scalar histories,
and many low-χ2 operator lists are nearly degenerate
with simple EH plus scalar baselines on FLRW. Con-

D. Scope of the background filter
The preceding analysis imposes two distinct
background-level requirements. First, Eq. (15) re-
moves all independent third- and higher-time derivatives
from the gauge-fixed FLRW equations. Second, the
residual time-reparametrization identity makes the
lapse equation a preserved first-order constraint on
the accepted branch. Together these conditions are
necessary for the background initial-value problem,
but they are not a sufficient proof of full covariant
ghost freedom. They do not establish the complete
ADM/Dirac constraint algebra, the quadratic action for
scalar and tensor perturbations, the sign of the kinetic
matrix, gradient stability, the tensor propagation speed,
or stability on non-FLRW backgrounds. These checks
are logically separate from the background search. In
this paper, “accepted” means that a branch has passed
the FLRW derivative-order filter, admits a regular
background initial-value problem, preserves the modified
Friedmann constraint numerically, satisfies the stated
background EFT cut, and yields a finite DES-SN plus
DESI-DR2 BAO score. It does not mean that the theory
is already a fully viable modified-gravity model.
On this restricted background sector, the modified
Friedmann constraint can be used to select initial data
and the remaining equations can be cast as the dimen-
sionless first-order system constructed next.

VI. AUTONOMOUS SEARCH STRATEGY
The autonomous component is an outer loop around
the deterministic physics pipeline. Each proposal is a
concrete theory JSON plus an explicit list of numeri-
cal parameter samples. The harness compiles the pro-
posal into an isolated Lagrangian file, runs the EOM and
Builder stages, evaluates the supplied samples with the
Solver, and writes one factual attempt record. The pro-
posal model then updates only from current-loop mem-
ory: failuretags, accepted-samplerows,χ 2 values, scalar-
family statistics, and parameter priors inferred from the
same run. Historical Solver winners are not used as cold-
start proposal memory.
The final benchmark uses two proposal sectors. The
open-search sector samples the full grammar and may
include the Einstein–Hilbert anchor. The non-EH sec-
tor forbids the bareRanchor and therefore searches for
backgrounds whose gravitational dynamics must be sup-
plied by curvature-squared, higher-curvature, derivative-
curvature, or scalar-curvature structures. The target ini-
tial proposal budget is split equally,
Nopen :N non-EH = 1 : 1.(44)
Expanded and refined validations do not count toward
this initial-theory target; they are repeat evaluations of a
promising already-proposed family with larger parameter
budgets.

VIII. VALIDATION BOUNDARIES AND
PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION
The validation performed here is a background-level
filter, not a complete proof of covariant viability. The
symbolic stage removes independent higher-time deriva-
tivesinthehomogeneousFLRWequationsandtheSolver
enforcesregularintegration, themodifiedFriedmanncon-
straint, and the data likelihood. A fully viable theory
must still pass perturbation-level tests: after solving non-
dynamical constraints, the scalar and tensor quadratic
actions must have positive kinetic eigenvalues, accept-
able sound speeds, no rapid gradient instabilities, and a
regular constraint structure. The present DES-SN plus
DESI-DR2 BAO likelihood also does not include struc-
ture growth, weak lensing, redshift-space distortions,
gravitational-wave propagation, or local-gravity screen-
ing.
The EFT interpretation is likewise diagnostic rather
than automatic. The trajectory-level cut controls|H|/Λ,
but broad coefficient and scalar priors can still produce
branches whose fitted corrections are large compared
with the EH term. Accepted operator lists also need not
be inequivalent covariant theories: integration by parts,

IX. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
The unified benchmark shows that autonomous pro-
posal agents can reliably recover high-quality EH-
anchored dark-energy backgrounds under a determinis-
tic modified-gravity validation pipeline. The best open-
search backgrounds improve the fixedΛCDM reference
by∆χ 2 ≃7.2–7.35, and the differences among the best
agents are small compared with the gap between open
search and the non-EH sector.
The non-EH results are more discriminating.
DeepSeek’sχ2
tot = 1693.860branch is the most in-
teresting new-physics candidate found in these data: it
is substantially better than the recurringχ2
tot ≃1946.87
plateau and is structurally simple. Nevertheless, it is still
worse than theΛCDM reference and much worse than
the EH-anchored basin. The current background data
and priors therefore favor EH-anchored scalar histories,
while non-EH backgrounds remain useful diagnostic
targets for further search.
